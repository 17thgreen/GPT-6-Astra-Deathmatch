# From a repeated reward gap to evidence of profit

The strongest current hypothesis is to detect newly funded, short reward windows
and supply a genuinely executable small-price bid where depth is missing. The
reward is compensation for making that order available. It is not free money,
a rebate equal to all exchange fees, or evidence of a trading edge by itself.

## What the research has established

- The broad reward catalog had 811 depth gaps among 5432 evaluable open markets.
- Ninety-one one-empty-side candidates passed a one-cent resting-price and ideal
  remaining-budget screen. This was a past snapshot, not a current trade list.
- Seven Miami hourly strikes showed high reward rates in one window; a separately
  frozen watch found the setup in seven new strikes in the next hour. Six passed
  all ten strict checks; one developed a one-contract competing bid.
- Some low-rate Truflation tails had full 1000-contract one-cent prints about
  three minutes after reward start, while the ideal accrued subsidy ceiling was
  only approximately five cents. The assumed absence of fills is untenable.
- Broad static threshold taking failed standard fees even where the gross price
  discrepancy was positive; no applicable active public volume reward was found.

## The decisive missing measurement

Our own quote changes the market. It may attract a taker who otherwise cannot
trade at that price, or cause another maker to compete. Therefore an empty-side
history with zero prints is not an estimate of fills after insertion. A public
snapshot cannot establish account eligibility, scored share or credited money.
A demo account can exercise mechanics but cannot establish real cash profitability.

Another passive repetition is not a substitute for this measurement. A
user-controlled live mechanics trial, if the user independently elects to conduct
one, would be needed to obtain actual fill and reward records. No such trial has
been authorized or performed by this research, and this document does not alter
existing financial risk limits or contain an automatic order-placement path.

## Reviewable mechanics-trial design

1. Before entry, retain the applicable current program terms, market rules,
   account eligibility, tick grid, fee terms, program ID, target, reward amount,
   start/end, order book and settlement source. Reject a quote that would cross.
2. Use a single market and a single unreplenished genuine order. The studied
   1000-at-one-cent example commits $10 of principal plus applicable fees; it is
   a scenario, not a new account risk limit. Do not multiply it across correlated
   strikes or automatically replace fills during this mechanics check.
3. Record the actual accepted order ID, timestamps, resting quantity, all partial
   fills, cancellation acknowledgement and any fills during cancellation. A
   post-only instruction prevents entry crossing; it does not prevent later fills.
4. Observe actual changes in competitor depth and qualified time. A partial fill
   can reduce total depth below target. Record pending or estimated rewards as
   unconfirmed; do not treat our 50% model as a payment quote.
5. Reconcile credited reward, purchase costs, fees, sales and final settlement
   for the tagged order. Net cash result = credited rewards + sale/settlement
   proceeds - purchase costs - all fees. Exclude deposits, unrelated positions
   and unresolved inventory from profit claims.
6. The first trial establishes or rejects scoring and ledger mechanics only.
   Any profitability study needs a new frozen sampling plan across fresh periods,
   keeping losses, failed admissions, partial fills and correlated exposures.
   Scale cannot be inferred from a single credited reward or a single quiet hour.

## Admission and rejection questions

- Does a completing one-cent quote actually receive the share implied by the
  public scoring description? For an empty side the ideal is 50% while both
  sides qualify. Existing orders, price-distance penalties and competition matter.
- Does net credited reward exceed filled-contract losses and fees across the
  prespecified sample, with a defensible uncertainty bound and capital accounting?
- Do campaigns appear frequently enough to justify the work, without extrapolating
  a sub-hour campaign's rate into permanent hourly income?
- Can a full adverse fill arrive before enough reward has accrued? At the observed
  Miami rate, one full $10 losing fill requires about 11.54 ideal minutes to offset;
  half that reward rate requires about 23.09 minutes. These are conditional cost
  thresholds, not forecasts of order lifetime.

Failure of reward eligibility/scoring, rapid competition, or inventory losses
consuming the subsidy is grounds to stop this lane. No required amount of research
can guarantee that a cash-cow strategy exists.
